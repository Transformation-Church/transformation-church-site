Paper for the church council

Replacing the church website

The rebuilt site is finished and running. This paper sets out what measurably changes, what carries over unchanged, and what is still outstanding before it can go live.

Transformation Church, Rowley Regis Registered charity 1208306 Prepared by Jobin Bennykutty

What is being asked

The proposal: retire the current WordPress site and point transformationchurch.co.uk at the rebuilt one.

Every page, sermon, photograph and policy from the current site has already been moved across. Nothing is discarded. The change is a switch of address, and it is reversible — the existing site can be restored by pointing the domain back.

What actually changes

Both home pages were loaded under identical conditions and measured with the same tooling. These are not estimates.

The speed difference matters most to the people we are least likely to hear from. A visitor on a weak mobile signal looking up what time the service starts currently waits around five seconds and downloads eight megabytes. Many will not wait.

Problems this fixes

Each of these was found on the current site during the rebuild.

The service times contradict themselves

The current site publishes four different versions of the Sunday schedule across its pages. A first-time visitor cannot tell which is right.

Now held in one place and used everywhere, including the live calendar.

Twelve links a screen reader cannot name

Twelve links on the current home page have no readable text, so a blind visitor hears only “link” with no indication of where it goes. Nine further elements fail contrast.

The rebuilt site passes every automated check on WCAG 2.2 AA across 24 pages on both desktop and mobile.

Advertising trackers load before anyone consents

On arrival the current site contacts Google Analytics, Google Tag Manager and DoubleClick, an advertising network, and sets cookies before any choice is offered.

The rebuilt site sets no cookies and loads nothing from third parties. Sermon videos only contact YouTube once someone presses play.

Six plugins to keep patched

The page builder, sermon manager, events calendar, SEO tool, cookie banner and security plugin each need updating, and each is a way in if it is not.

The rebuilt site has no plugins and no database to compromise. Pages are pre-built files.

A vacancy nobody can find

The Youth Pastor job description sits in the current site's media library with no page linking to it, and closes on 30 September.

Now a proper page, written so it can appear in Google’s job listings.

Statements of faith missing their references

The thirteen beliefs on the current About page have had their scripture citations stripped out.

Restored in full from the original document, every reference in place.

What the church gains

A live What’s On page

Reads directly from the church’s own ChurchSuite calendar. Whatever the office puts in the diary appears on the website within fifteen minutes, with no one touching the site.

Giving, with Gift Aid explained

Four ways to give, and a plain explanation that Gift Aid adds 25p to every pound at no cost to the giver. The current site has no giving page at all.

The blog is editable without a developer

Posts are written and published through a simple editor. No code, no plugin updates, and a mistake cannot break the rest of the site.

Findable by search engines and AI assistants

The service times, address, charity number and every sermon are published in a form search engines read directly, so an answer to “what time is the service” comes from us rather than from guesswork.

How these figures were produced. Both sites were loaded in the same browser at the same screen size with an empty cache, and timings taken from the browser’s own performance record. Accessibility was tested with axe, the industry-standard automated checker, against WCAG 2.2 Level AA.

Two honest caveats. Each site was measured once from one location, so the timings will vary a little between runs; the scale of the difference will not. And automated accessibility testing catches most problems but not all — it is not a substitute for testing with people who use assistive technology.

The rebuilt site can be reviewed in full before any decision is taken. Every point in this paper can be demonstrated live.
